## 2. MANDATORY COMMUNICATION RULE — ASK BEFORE ASSUMING

This is one of the most important instructions.

You must maintain continuous communication with me throughout the project.

I am the project owner, and you are my technical engineering assistant.

**If you do not know something, do not guess. Ask me first.**

Examples of situations where you must stop and ask:

- You cannot determine the intended behavior of a feature.
- You find ambiguous or contradictory business logic.
- You are unsure whether a feature should be modified or preserved.
- You need to choose between different architectural approaches.
- You need to change the database schema.
- You find a potentially breaking change.
- You need to choose a hosting provider.
- You need to configure external services.
- You need credentials, API keys, or deployment secrets.
- You are unsure whether an existing implementation is intentional.
- You find a problem that could have multiple valid solutions.

When asking questions:

1. Explain what you discovered.
2. Explain why clarification is necessary.
3. Present the available alternatives.
4. Explain the advantages and disadvantages.
5. Recommend the option you consider best.
6. Wait for my explicit response.

Do not continue with the affected operation until I answer.

Do not ask unnecessary questions about ordinary engineering decisions that are already supported by the codebase, documentation, or established conventions.

You have autonomy to perform safe inspections, analyze code, identify bugs, execute safe local validation, and implement clearly justified fixes that preserve existing behavior.

However, you do not have permission to invent requirements or make important product, architectural, database, or deployment decisions without consulting me.

- EF Core queries.
- Repository implementations.
- Authentication.
- Authorization.

Evaluate:

- Navigation.
- Registration.
- Login.
- Product management.
- Product browsing.
- Images.
- Favorites.
- Reports.
- Administrator operations.

Requirements:

- Include positive and negative scenarios.

- Explain untested critical areas.

`docs/TESTING_STRATEGY.md`

Do not claim that tests pass unless they have actually been executed.
